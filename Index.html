    Pattern library

    Six scams the scanner is built around

    Each one follows a script. The detector looks for the parts of the script, so it can explain a score instead of just announcing one.

    Wrong-transfer reversal

    • "I sent money to your number by mistake"
    • Pressure to send it back quickly
    • A sad story about a hospital or an emergency

    What to doCheck your own balance and transaction history. Do not return money because of a text.

    Account suspension and PIN theft

    • Claims your SIM, wallet or card is blocked
    • Asks for a PIN, OTP or password
    • Links to a look-alike website

    What to doProviders do not ask for your PIN by message. Contact them through their official channels.

    Fake jobs and fees

    • High pay for easy work from home
    • A "registration" or "processing" fee
    • Contact only through WhatsApp

    What to doReal employers do not charge you to be hired. Verify the company independently.

    Prize and promo claims

    • "Congratulations, you have won"
    • A competition you never entered
    • A small fee to release the prize

    What to doIf you did not enter, you did not win. Delete and move on.

    Investment doubling

    • Guaranteed returns in days
    • Screenshots of other people "cashing out"
    • Pressure to bring in friends

    What to doNo legitimate investment guarantees profit. Ask whether the seller is licensed.

    Authority impersonation

    • Threats of arrest or fines
    • Claims to be an agency, bank or officer
    • "Do not tell anyone about this"

    What to doHang up and call the organisation using a number you looked up yourself.

    Method

    How a message becomes a score

    The current prototype is a transparent, rule-based model. Every point of the score traces back to a phrase you can see highlighted.

    STEP 1

    Input

    A pasted message. Processed locally, never stored.

    STEP 2

    Pattern matching

    Regular expressions find requests for PINs, fees, threats and urgency.

    STEP 3

    Link analysis

    Shortened links, risky domain endings and brand names inside odd addresses.

    STEP 4

    Risk scoring

    Signal weights combine so that several weak signs outweigh one.

    STEP 5

    Explanation

    Highlighted phrases, plain reasons and a recommended next step.

    risk = 100 × ( 1 − ∏ (1 − wᵢ) ) where wᵢ is the weight of each distinct signal found, between 0 and 1

      Limits and roadmap

      What this prototype does not claim

      A fraud tool that overstates itself becomes a fraud risk. These are the boundaries of the current build.

      Known limitations

      • Rules catch known scripts. A new wording can score low, so a low score is never proof that a message is safe.
      • Legitimate messages that mention PINs or fees may score higher than they should.
      • Phrases are matched in English. Bemba, Nyanja and mixed-language messages need their own patterns.
      • The board is stored in your own browser for this demo. A real deployment needs a shared, moderated database.
      • Example reports on the board are illustrative and are not real incidents.

      Roadmap

      PHASE 1

      Working prototypeExplainable scanner and community board, as shown here.

      PHASE 2

      Labelled dataset and ML modelCollect consented reports, label them, train a classifier and measure precision and recall against the rule-based baseline.

      PHASE 3

      Moderation and integrationAdmin review of reports, multilingual support and a path to share findings with network operators and banks.
